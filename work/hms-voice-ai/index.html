HMS Voice AI — the doctor dictates, the system writes

Flagship · Healthcare AI

NeGD (MeitY) · HMS hospital platform — Voice AI designed, built & integrated by Shahrukh

The use case: a doctor finishes a patient round and simply speaks — in the Hindi-English mix (Hinglish) she actually uses. The structured clinical note appears on screen for her confirmation. No typing, no templates, no data leaving the hospital.

The scenario, as implemented

  • 1 · Dictate — the doctor dictates from the browser voice panel: symptoms, vitals, diagnosis, medicines, advice — in Hindi, Hinglish or English.
  • 2 · Transcribe — LiveKit streams the audio (WebRTC); faster-whisper transcribes it on the hospital's own server.
  • 3 · Structure — MedGemma 4B (Google's medical LLM, self-hosted on Ollama) turns the transcript into a structured note — fields, doses and vitals in their right places.
  • 4 · Confirm — the doctor reviews and confirms on screen. Nothing is ever saved without clinician confirmation — the confirm-gate is architectural, not a setting.
  • 5 · Save & speak back — the note saves through the existing HMS service APIs, and Kokoro TTS speaks the confirmation summary back to the doctor.

From POC to hospital-wide

  • Proved as an isolated POC alongside the live system — live data untouched — and validated module-by-module across EMR, OPD, Pathology, Radiology, Pharmacy, Billing, IPD and Discharge on Hinglish dictation.
  • Then integrated into every clinical module of the 20-microservice HMS — EMR doctor notes, IPD rounds and nurse notes, ICU, OT, radiology, pathology and discharge — behind the platform's JWT gateway.
  • Performance work centred on the inference path: keeping the model warm, trimming decoding parameters and parallelising transcription, until dictation felt like a tool rather than a wait.
  • Every model is free and open-source, self-hosted — patient audio is processed inside the hospital's own servers and never leaves. Every component is free and open-source.

KEY DECISIONS & ALTERNATIVES
Self-hosted open-source models, not cloud AI APIsCloud speech/LLM APIs were rejected on data-residency grounds: patient audio must never leave the hospital. Whisper + MedGemma + Kokoro run on hospital hardware, with no licence cost and no external API in the path.
Confirm-before-save as architectureThe clinician-confirmation gate is built into the data path, not a UI preference — nothing reaches the record unreviewed.
Isolated POC firstThe stack proved itself in a separate compose project and database beside the live HMS; only validated modules were integrated. A larger model (Qwen3-8B) was evaluated and rejected — it exceeded the ~6 GB POC memory budget.
LIMITATIONS & LESSONS
Performance is an inference problemThe latency work lived in the inference path: keeping the model warm, trimming decoding parameters, parallelising transcription on a CPU-only host. A GPU node is the documented scaling path when volume demands it.
Known limitationWhisper can garble Hindi numerals in fast speech — the editable transcript review exists precisely for this.
LessonIn clinical AI, the trust feature is the gate, not the model. Adoption followed the confirm step, not the demo.
